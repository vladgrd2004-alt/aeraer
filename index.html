    @VLADISLAVVOVV

    For this womenswear project, I wanted to explore the

    intersection of cinematic atmosphere and structural

    design. The concept is deeply inspired by the dystopian,

    cyberpunk aesthetic of the film Blade Runner 2049.

    I was captivated by the movie’s dark, rainy, and

    mysterious world, which is strikingly illuminated by

    bright neon lights.

    My primary goal was to translate and capture this

    specific mood into a tangible garment.

    To achieve this, I turned to the philosophy of Issey

    Miyake, a designer who was a true master of technology

    in fashion. Rather than simply making clothes, he

    essentially designed “architecture for the body”.

    I was highly inspired by his approach—specifically, how

    his garments move and continuously change shape

    depending on the wearer.

    The final piece is a dark, enigmatic women’s ensemble

    that deliberately features a non-standard silhouette.

    The focal point of the outfit is the structured,

    hooded element, where I utilized Miyake’s signature

    pleating technique to craft the cape.

    The structured, pleated hood provides a sense of

    mystery and protection from a harsh outside world,

    partially concealing the face to emphasize an

    atmosphere of isolation.

    Meanwhile, the long, flowing dress underneath

    contrasts this rigidity with dark, fluid elegance. By

    combining architectural pleats with dynamic movement,

    the design reflects a balance between cold futuristic

    technology and human vulnerability.

    Thesis, page 5

    1.1 INTRODUCTION

    AËR Elegance views clothing as a form of

    lightweight engineering. The collection takes the

    airship—a structure that, despite its enormous size,

    is light enough to float in the air—as its inspiration

    and applies that logic to the world of fashion.

    Futurism offers the dream of flight, steampunk provides visible

    structure, sculptural lines of draping.

    The result is a wearable aesthetic: structure and lightness coexist

    here, and the memory of a long-lost flying machine is carried over

    into contemporary clothing.

    •

    5

    Thesis, page 6

    The AËR Elegance collection operates as a material and structural

    thesis—an architectural argument articulated through cloth, internal

    boning, and aerodynamic volume. Rather than relying on a single

    historical costume reference or decorative retro-stylization, the

    project investigates how two seemingly opposing cultural paradigms—

    nineteenth-century mechanical engineering (reinterpreted through

    Steampunk) and early twentieth-century Italian Futurism—can converge

    within contemporary garment construction.

    At the core of this inquiry lies a fundamental engineering paradox

    embodied by the rigid airship: how an object of monumental scale can

    achieve weightlessness, structural stability, and fluid motion through

    the atmosphere. In fashion design, clothing traditionally negotiates

    between two poles: rigid tailoring that imposes form upon the body,

    and fluid drapery that surrenders to gravity. AËR Elegance rejects this

    binary.

    • Rather than relying on a single established style, the

    project explores how these trends can work together in

    terms of cut, material treatment, and construction

    •

    6

    Thesis, page 7

    By studying the internal duralumin frames and stretched outer

    fabric envelopes of historic airships alongside modern hybrid

    aerostats, this research embodies the principles of aeronautics

    in wearable silhouettes.

    Here, clothing is perceived not merely as superficial ornamentation, but as a self-supporting vessel—a lightweight engineered

    habitat in which visible structural frames and sculptural,

    air-filled volumes coexist in dynamic equilibrium.

    Thesis, page 8

    1.2 ENGINEERING AERODYNAMICS

    Aerodynamics is the science of the interaction between gases

    and moving objects, in which a delicate balance exists between

    four forces: lift, weight, thrust, and drag. In the early stages of

    aeronautical engineering, designers discovered that to overcome

    air resistance, it was necessary to move away from angular,

    boxy structures in favor of smooth, streamlined shapes—teardrop-shaped curves, tapered tail sections, and smooth elliptical

    cross-sections. Every seam, stiffener, and surface tension on

    the aircraft’s fuselage was calculated so that the airflow would

    smoothly flow around its surface without creating turbulence.

    The clothing’s design is inspired by the geometry of airships and

    aircraft. Streamlined silhouettes, soft curves, and an anatomical cut

    mimic air resistance and the dynamics of flight.

    •

    8

    Thesis, page 9

    In the AËR Elegance collection, these aerodynamic principles

    are directly embodied in the creation of patterns and the

    anatomical cut. Rather than treating volume as a heavy, static

    mass, the silhouettes are designed to occupy space with

    minimal visual weight. Curved princess seams, radial pleats,

    and graduated padding mimic the lines of airflow around the

    fuselage, smoothly guiding the viewer’s gaze along the body’s

    vertical axis. Just as an airship’s outer envelope must remain

    taut despite changing atmospheric pressure, the fabrics in this

    collection are treated with heat-setting, structural elements,

    and internal padding to maintain crisp, taut contours while

    remaining flexible and responsive to the wearer’s movements

    1.3 STEAMPUNK

    Steampunk emerged as an aesthetic protest and an expression of

    nostalgia for a time when machinery was visible.

    Contrary to the notion of crude mechanics, steampunk is visually

    very poetic and fluid:

    The Geometry of Air: Shapes inspired by aeronautics—perfect

    spheres, ovals, and the streamlined silhouettes of airships, hot-air

    balloons, and bathyscaphes.

    Surrealism: a fusion of organic and mechanical elements; silhouettes

    often appear somewhat phantasmagorical and exaggerated.

    STEAMPUNK - IS A SCIENCE FICTION GENRE AND

    STYLE BASED ON AN ALTERNATE HISTORY IN WHICH

    19TH-CENTURY STEAM ENGINES AND MECHANICS HAVE

    REACHED A HIGH LEVEL OF DEVELOPMENT.

    Thesis, page 11

    HOW STEAMPUNK SHAPED

    THE COLLECTION

    • In the AËR Elegance collection, the “steampunk” style was

    stripped of theatrical clichés (such as decorative gears or

    Victorian cosplay) and reduced to three rigorous design methodologies: “Structural Transparency”: Inspired by exposed beams

    and external cables, the ribs of the corset, stitched channels, and

    structural seams are brought to the garment’s outer surface as

    primary graphic elements rather than hidden internal supports.

    Unlike purely functional industrial design, the steampunk aesthetic

    leans toward smooth, rounded, and slightly surreal forms inspired

    by early aviation—such as hot-air balloons, airships, and spherical

    instruments.

    •

    11

    Thesis, page 12

    A key feature of this style is its structural openness: visible frames, exposed

    supports, and intricate details are not hidden but, on the contrary, are

    emphasized as integral elements of the artistic form.

    Although steampunk is associated with heavy machinery and mechanics,

    it conveys a strong sense of lightness and airiness, evoking the dream of

    flight.

    •

    12

    Thesis, page 13

    By borrowing its iconic rounded forms, earthy palette, and visible

    construction details, I translated this idea of airiness and early

    aviation into a refined, wearable aesthetic.

    Thesis, page 14

    STEAMPUNK IN CINEMA: VISUAL

    CODES, VICTORIAN MECHANICS,AERIAL ARCHITECTURE.

    Within the context of contemporary visual culture, cinema has played a

    decisive role in transforming steampunk from a niche literary subculture into a

    globally recognized design language.

    Unlike sterile futuristic films, where cutting-edge technologies are depicted

    as smooth, impenetrable surfaces, cinematic steampunk emphasizes tactile

    clarity.

    •

    14

    Thesis, page 15

    Historically, cinematic steampunk has drawn heavily on the material culture

    of the Victorian and Edwardian eras of the 19th century: industrial ironwork,

    early prototypes of flying machines, woolen suits, corseted silhouettes, and

    precision timepieces.

    However, the most influential films in this tradition do not merely evoke

    historical nostalgia; on the contrary, they view 19th-century mechanics as an

    alternative present—a parallel technological timeline in which human craftsmanship and monumental engineering coexist.

    For AËR Elegance, this cinematic tradition serves as an important methodological bridge: it demonstrates how massive mechanical frameworks and

    delicate, flowing outer layers can be united into a single poetic silhouette.

    •

    15

    Thesis, page 16

    Hayao Miyazaki’s film *Howl’s Moving Castle* (Studio Ghibli, 2004),

    loosely based on Diana Wynne Jones’s 1986 novel, is one of the most subtle

    cinematic embodiments of the steampunk aesthetic.

    Rather than portraying machinery as cold, monotonous industrial standardization, Miyazaki and art directors Yoji Takeshige and Noboru Yoshida created

    a hybrid European world modeled after 19th-century Alsatian towns (such

    as Colmar and Riquewihr) combined with Belle Époque urbanism, steam

    locomotives, and early aviation.

    Thesis, page 20

    In Howl’s Moving Castle, the skies are patrolled by heavy military airships,

    flying machines resembling ornithopters, and airships with balloon-like hulls,

    visually reminiscent of the rigid airships and experimental aircraft of the late

    19th and early 20th centuries.

    What makes Miyazaki’s approach to the theme of flight particularly relevant

    to the AËR Elegance collection is the contrast between armor and air. The

    aircraft in the film combine exposed metal frames, propellers, and riveted

    gondolas with enormous inflatable fabric envelopes billowing against the

    backdrop of the sky.

    Transposed into the architecture of the garments, this duality directly reflects

    1.4 FUTURISM

    Italian Futurism, which emerged in 1909 with the

    publication of Filippo Tommaso Marinetti’s “Futurist

    Manifesto” in the newspaper *Le Figaro*, called for a

    radical break with static academic traditions, championing

    dynamism, technological speed, the aesthetics of

    machines, and the conquest of the skies.

    Over the course of a decade, Futurist artists and

    designers—primarily Giacomo Balla and Ernesto Michelles

    (Tayacht)—extended these principles to clothing, asserting

    that modern attire should reject symmetry, restrained

    bourgeois passivity, and static heaviness in favor of

    kinetic energy, bold geometric planes, and aerodynamic

    ergonomics.

    In fashion and visual art, futuristic design is defined

    by dynamic lines, clean geometric shapes, ergonomic

    silhouettes, and a sense of constant movement.

    In film, futurism is rarely achieved solely through technological gadgets or

    visual effects; it becomes truly believable when the spatial setting, physical

    movements, and clothing begin to behave differently than in everyday life.

    Costumes play a crucial role in creating this believability, as they fit directly

    against the body and reveal how a character moves, shifts weight, maintains

    posture, and exists within a hypothetical or futuristic world. Through their cut,

    tension, and the resistance of the fabric, the clothing on screen transforms an

    abstract world-building concept into a tangible physical experience.

    A brief analysis of the films “Blade Runner”, “Star Wars”, and “The Fifth Element”

    helps identify specific visual and structural principles that resonate with my

    own design practice. Rather than attempting to construct an exhaustive history

    particular attention to the subtle nuances in the proportions of clothing, layered

    construction, and structural articulation that define how form, structural

    support, and volume evolve in the AËR Elegance collection.

    1.7 WHY I CHOSE THE AIRSHIP AS

    THE MAIN THEME

    I chose airships as the central theme for the AËR Elegance collection because I

    have long been drawn to engineering, particularly structural design, aerodynamics,

    and the creation of aircraft. Rigid airships stand out as a remarkable type of airship,

    where monumental, large-scale volumes are supported by a thoughtful internal

    framework, rather than solely by gas pressure.

    This coexistence of intricate engineering, load-bearing structure, and a distinct

    sense of weightlessness or lightness makes airships a compelling source of

    inspiration for contemporary fashion design.

    Rather than viewing flight purely as a decorative motif, this exploration delves into

    how an aircraft of immense physical proportions can evoke a sense of buoyancy,

    grace, and aerial suspension. In AËR Elegance, this fundamental tension between

    structural discipline and airy lightness becomes the conceptual anchor for

    reimagining how garments hold volume around the body without feeling heavy or

    static.

    Fascinated by aviation, I decided to explore how the engineering logic

    of airships can be embodied in wearable clothing through silhouette,

    inner structure, proportions and surface details. In traditional knitting

    and drape, clothing often oscillates between a rigid structure that

    restrains the body and a soft fabric that is completely subject to

    gravity; the design of airships offers a new perspective on this

    balance.

    In the collection, this is embodied in structured corsets and a corset

    bones that serve as a carcass, combined with pleated, tapered,

    and multilayer fabric volumes resembling tight outer shells and

    volumetric contours. Each line of suture, change of proportions and

    structural part is seen as a way to express form and movement on the

    body while maintaining visual lightness and responsiveness of the

    silhouette.

    1.8 ABOUT PROJECT

    The main goal of AËR Elegance is to bring together

    several distinct stylistic and historical influences,

    including Italian Futurism, Steampunk aesthetics, and

    industrial engineering, particularly as represented by

    the construction of early aircraft and airships. These

    references belong to different periods and artistic

    contexts, yet they share an interest in technological

    progress, structural innovation, and the possibilities

    of human imagination. The project explores how these

    different influences can coexist within one contemporary fashion collection, forming a consistent visual

    and conceptual identity.

    •

    77

    Thesis, page 78

    Rather than using these references as direct visual

    quotations or reproducing historical styles, I aim to

    reinterpret their fundamental principles through the

    language of fashion design

    . I am interested in the way engineering solutions can

    become aesthetic elements, and how ideas originally

    developed for mechanical structures can be translated

    into the proportions, construction, and visual character of a

    garment.

    The intention is to move beyond decoration and create a

    collection in which every design element contributes to the

    overall concept.

    •

    78

    Thesis, page 79

    A central part of this research is the relationship between

    structure and movement. The silhouettes explore the contrast

    between controlled, architectural forms and softer, more fluid

    volumes. Through sculptural shapes and carefully considered

    proportions, the garments are intended to suggest the presence

    of an engineered object while remaining connected to the human

    body. This approach allows the collection to investigate how

    clothing can communicate strength and stability while maintaining

    a sense of lightness and movement.

    Ultimately, I want AËR Elegance to express the atmosphere of

    flight through contemporary garment design. The collection is

    not intended to represent aircraft literally, but to reinterpret the

    visual impression they create: their scale, unusual proportions,

    mechanical complexity, and ability to appear almost weightless. By

    combining historical inspiration with an individual design approach,

    the project aims to establish a distinctive aesthetic in which

    engineering, fashion, and the imagination of flight become part of

    one coherent language.

    1.1 INTRODUCTION

    I have long been drawn to engineering, structural

    design, and the creation of aircraft. Rigid airships

    stand out because monumental volumes are

    supported by a thoughtful internal framework rather

    than gas pressure alone, creating a striking sense of

    weightlessness and lightness.

    Although airships have almost disappeared from

    everyday life, this project preserves and reinterprets

    their engineering logic as contemporary fashion.

    1.2 ABOUT THE PROJECT

    AËR Elegance approaches clothing as a form of

    lightweight engineering—a self-supporting lightness

    characterized by structural frameworks and

    sculptural volumes that appear as if filled with air, all

    existing in a state of equilibrium. The collection fuses

    19th-century mechanical engineering, Steampunk,

    and Futurism into contemporary garment forms.
